Engineering notebooks let you document your project's journey throughout a HackerSquad event. Track your iterations from initial idea through final implementation, giving judges and other attendees insight into your thought process and technical decisions.

<Tip>Engineering notebooks help judges understand your thought process and can positively influence your score. A well-maintained notebook shows depth of thinking beyond just the final product.</Tip>

## What are engineering notebooks?

An engineering notebook is a structured log of your project's development. Rather than just submitting a finished project, notebooks capture the story of how you got there — what problems you identified, what approaches you tried, and how your solution evolved.

Notebooks are organized into **iterations**, each containing **comments** categorized by type. This structure mirrors the engineering design process and helps you reflect on your work as you build.

## Creating a notebook

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the event page">
    Navigate to the event you're participating in and look for the **Engineering Notebooks** section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create a new notebook">
    Click **Create Notebook** and give it a title — typically your project name or the problem you're solving.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define the problem">
    Add your first entry describing the problem you're tackling. Use the **Problem** comment type to frame what you're trying to solve.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document as you build">
    As you work through the event, add comments to track your progress, decisions, and learnings. Use the appropriate comment type for each entry.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Comment types

Each comment in your notebook is categorized by type to provide structure:

| Comment type | When to use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General** | General notes, observations, or context | "Found an interesting API that could simplify our data layer" |
| **Problem** | Defining the problem or identifying a sub-problem | "Users can't easily discover relevant events in their area" |
| **Approach** | Describing a strategy or solution design | "We'll use geolocation filtering combined with interest tags" |
| **Implementation** | Documenting code decisions and technical specifics | "Switched from REST to WebSocket for real-time leaderboard updates" |

## Tracking iterations

Notebooks are organized by iterations — distinct phases of your project's development. Each iteration can contain multiple comments of different types.

A typical notebook progression looks like:

- **Iteration 1** — Problem research and brainstorming. Define the problem and explore potential approaches.
- **Iteration 2** — First prototype. Document your initial implementation, what worked, and what didn't.
- **Iteration 3** — Refinement. Record pivots, optimizations, and improvements based on testing or feedback.
- **Iteration 4** — Final polish. Capture last-minute changes, demo prep, and reflections on what you learned.

<Tip>You don't need to wait until an iteration is "done" to start adding comments. Document your thinking as it happens — stream-of-consciousness entries are valuable too.</Tip>

## How notebooks help you

Engineering notebooks benefit you in several ways:

- **Better judging outcomes** — judges can see the depth of your engineering thinking, not just the polished result
- **Team alignment** — if you're working with a team, the notebook keeps everyone on the same page about decisions and reasoning
- **Personal growth** — reviewing your notebook after the event helps you identify what you learned and how you approach problems
- **Portfolio piece** — a well-written notebook demonstrates your engineering process to potential collaborators or employers

## What judges look for

When judges review engineering notebooks, they typically evaluate:

- **Problem understanding** — did you clearly define the problem you're solving?
- **Technical depth** — do your approaches show thoughtful engineering decisions?
- **Iteration quality** — did you adapt and improve as the event progressed?
- **Documentation clarity** — is your thought process easy to follow?
