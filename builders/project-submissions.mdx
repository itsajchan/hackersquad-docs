Submitting a project is how you showcase what you've built during an event. Your submission includes a description of your work, a link to your code, demo details, and the technologies you used.

## What's in a project submission

Each project submission includes the following fields:

| Field | Required | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Project name** | Yes | A short, descriptive name for your project |
| **Description** | Yes | An overview of what your project does and the problem it solves |
| **GitHub link** | Optional | A link to your project's source code repository |
| **Demo description** | Optional | A written explanation of how your project works and what it demonstrates |
| **Technologies used** | Optional | The sponsor companies and tools you used in your build |
| **Request a demo** | Optional | Toggle this to request a live demo slot during the event |
| **Judging partner opt-in** | Optional | Allow sponsor companies whose tools you used to contact you after the event |
| **Team members** | Optional | Email addresses of teammates to associate with the project |

## How to submit

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the event page">
    Navigate to the event you're attending and go to the **Projects** section.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create your project">
    Click **Submit Project** and fill in your project name and description.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your GitHub link">
    Paste the URL to your project's repository so judges and attendees can review your code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a demo description">
    Describe how your project works, what it does, and what makes it interesting. This is what judges and voters will read if they can't see a live demo.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select technologies used">
    Tag the sponsor companies and tools you used during the event. This helps organizers track technology adoption and connects you with sponsors.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Request a demo slot (optional)">
    If you'd like to present your project live, toggle the **Request a demo** option. Event organizers will review and approve demo requests.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Opt in to judging partner contact (optional)">
    Enable this if you're open to sponsor companies reaching out to you after the event. This is a great way to build connections with companies whose tools you used.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add team members (optional)">
    Enter the email addresses of your teammates to link them to the project submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit">
    Review your details and submit your project. You can edit your submission until the event deadline.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>You can update your submission at any time before the deadline. Add your GitHub link and demo description as your project progresses — you don't need everything ready when you first submit.</Tip>

## Video recordings

Some events support video demo recordings. If available, you can record a short video walkthrough of your project that judges and attendees can watch asynchronously. The recording link will appear on your project submission after it's been captured during a live demo session.

## Team submissions

If you're working with a team, only one member needs to submit the project. Add your teammates' email addresses during submission to link everyone to the project. The team leader typically handles the submission, but any team member can edit it.

See [Teams](/builders/teams) for details on forming and managing teams.

## After submitting

Once your project is submitted:

- **Judges** can review your project details and score your work
- **Attendees** can view your submission and vote for it during community voting (if enabled)
- **Sponsors** can see which of their tools you used and may reach out if you opted in
- **Event organizers** can manage demo scheduling based on your demo request

Check the event's [leaderboard](/builders/demos-and-voting) to see how your project ranks as scores and votes come in.
